## D1: coverage-fair fusion (Y4.2-D1)

**Notation.**
- O = the search's own-text lists, n_O of them. For a typed search these are own dense and own BM25. For more-like-this they are the own centroid and own BM25 on the examples' text.
- T = the te lists, n_T of them: te BM25 and te dense / te centroid.
- r_l(i) = the 1-based rank of item i in list l. An item absent from a list adds no term. Lists are truncated at depth 1,000, as before.
- RRF_X(i) = Σ_{l∈X} 1 / (60 + r_l(i)).
- E = items with a rankable te row: 27,304 (Instagram 20,828; YouTube 6,476).

**Formula.**

  F(i) = RRF_O(i) + RRF_T(i)                  if i ∈ E
  F(i) = RRF_O(i) · (1 + n_T / n_O)           if i ∉ E

**What it does.**
- An item without a te row gets, for each te list it cannot appear in, the stand-in contribution of its own lists. With n_O = n_T = 2 this is the mean over the item's two views, with the own view standing in for the missing te view.
- Enriched items keep C1's relative order, and unenriched items keep C0's.
- An item ranked first everywhere scores 4/61 with or without a te row. In C1, an unenriched item's maximum was 2/61.
- A more-like-this search whose examples have no te rows has n_T = 0, so F = RRF_O, exactly as in C0.
- E is a per-item property, so D1 is platform-blind.

## D1p: te ranks on the corpus scale (Y4.2-D1p)

**The problem.** The te lists rank only the 27,304 te rows. A te rank r is therefore a much better percentile than an own-list rank r over about 120k items, so enriched items still collect cheap mid-list ranks under D1.

**The change.** D1p replaces each te-list term 1/(60 + r) with 1/(60 + r · N_own / N_te). The scales are fixed per index, not per search:

| te list | N_own | Scale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| te BM25 | 122,330 items with view text | 4.48 |
| te dense | 115,931 gated vectors | 4.25 |

Own lists and the imputation are as in D1.

## D2: per-item sufficiency routing (Y4.2-D2)

**Routed set.** An item is routed if it is **insufficient** (round 4.1's C2 rule, unchanged: no own text, info class `non_english`, or fewer than 4 content words) **and** has a rankable te-v1c row.

**For each routed item:**
- its BM25 text becomes own view + " " + te text;
- its vector becomes text-embedding-3-small 512 d of that merged text (store `corpus/emb/d2_merged_te`, keyed by item code and text);
- it is gated into the dense leg.

**Unchanged.** Sufficient items and items without a te row are as in C0. There are no te lists, so every item has exactly C0's lists. More-like-this uses the same per-item text and vectors.

## D3: guarded qi-v3 intent on C0 (Y4.2-D3)

**The guards.** These are applied to the cached, validated qi-v3 intent and use only the search text and the qi-v3 `kind`. Exact word lists are in LOG.md Y4.2-D3 and in `c4_intent.py` (`TIME_RX`, `FLAG_RX`, `NEG_RX`, `ORDER_TIME_RX`, `MEDIA`).
1. **Date window:** kept only with an explicit time word (a year, a month, a season, "last …", "… ago", "this week/month/…", "yesterday").
2. **Behaviour flags:**
   - `require[x]` is kept only if x's own word is present;
   - `exclude[x]` is kept only if x's word and a negation are both present;
   - watch-count bounds and `sort most_watched` are kept only with a watch word;
   - `sort newest/oldest` is kept only with a time or recency word.
3. **Watch data is unknown, not false:**
   - `watched` is dropped from `require` on platforms without watch data (the item passes if it was the only flag);
   - `exclude watched` and watch-count bounds pass on those platforms;
   - a window check there counts any interaction.
4. **Platform** is kept only when the search names it (round 4.1's guard).
5. **topic_free** (behaviour-only ordering) is honoured only if the kind has neither topic nor vibe **and** no content word is left after removing STOP, behaviour, time and media words.

Duration and Shorts fields are unchanged.

**Deviation, before scoring (recorded in LOG.md).**
- Rule 3 was preregistered as "a platform with no watch interactions in the corpus". The first d3/d4 run meta showed Instagram counted as having watch data: the export has watch events on 216 of 21,293 Instagram items (1.0%), against 94.0% for YouTube. The guard could therefore never fire.
- The rule was amended to "a platform has watch data when ≥ 50% of its items carry a watch interaction". Under that rule YouTube has watch data and Instagram does not.
- d3 and d4 were rerun. The first files were overwritten and never scored.

**Effect of the guards.** Counts are over the 47 typed searches per repeat (more-like-this searches skip the intent step).

- Hard-filter pools that change a ranking range from 3,439 to 129,978 items.

## D4: D1 + D3 (Y4.2-D4)

**Rule.** D4 uses D1 unless D1's label-free cross-platform share of the yt_-search top 10 exceeds 0.35. The rule was fixed in LOG.md before any run. D1's share is **0.29**, so D4 = D1 + D3, as preregistered.

**Design reason.**
- The only measured te gain (C1 on Instagram, +0.05 to +0.08 in both of round 4.1's projections) is te on *all* Instagram items.
- D1 keeps that gain and removes the extra-vote confound.
- D2 drops te for the 75% of Instagram items that are sufficient, and its routing gain is untested.
