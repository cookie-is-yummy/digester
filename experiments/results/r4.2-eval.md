## Method

- **Scoring.**
  - All 18 round-4.2 files, plus 4.1's `c0`, `c1` and `c3`, were scored on dev: 44 searches, 22 `ig` and 22 `yt`.
  - Each search's metrics are averaged over the 3 rewrite repeats.
  - CIs are 2,000-sample bootstraps over searches. Comparisons pair repeat k with repeat k, average each search's Δ over the repeats, then bootstrap. W/L/T uses |Δ| > 0.005. All as in round 4.1.
  - Per-kind rows count a search under every kind it carries, so they overlap.
- **Control check.**
  - `c0x` equals 4.1's `c0`: top 10 identical in 165 of 165 search × repeat cells, top 50 in 162 of 165.
  - Every metric's Δ is 0.000 overall and per scope.
  - Paired comparisons use `c0x`; the numbers are identical against `c0`.
- **Pooling.**
  - Every unjudged dev top-10 item across all 4.2 runs (and `c0`, `c1`) was pooled: 81 items over 26 searches.
  - 52 were Instagram-scope items (31 of them YouTube items); 29 were YouTube-scope items (5 of them Instagram items).
  - Seed unjudged@10 was already low (0.00–0.06). The 4.2 arms mostly reorder items 4.1 had judged.
  - Backup before the merge: `evalset.pre_r4.2.jsonl`.
- **Blind judging, using 4.1's conventions.**
  - The sheet was built with 4.1's builder (copied to `eval_work/r4.2/tools/`). It is grouped by search and shuffled within each search, with no run identity or rank shown.
  - Text shown: own text, interactions and duration. The video-pass description was shown only for untitled YouTube items.
  - **te text was not shown**, as in rounds 1 and 4.1. This slightly disfavours the te arms.
  - Items were graded by meaning, not by platform. Short-form items lost a grade where the intent asks for long videos.

**The I17 loss is a guard design flaw, not an intent error.**
- `require` flags are ORed: C3's saved+shared pool was larger than saved alone.
- Dropping one flag therefore narrows the pool. The search names both behaviours, but sharing in words a synonym that the share lexicon lacks ("sending to someone").
- A guard should only ever widen: if any flag of a multi-flag `require` fails its word check, drop the whole `require`. And the share lexicon should cover sending.
- **Counterfactual** (evaluator-side, D4 with I17 at D1's ranking): +0.056 [+0.010, +0.105], 30/8 vs C0.

### D3's watch-data deviation

- **What changed.**
  - Rule 3 was preregistered as "a platform with no watch interactions in the corpus". Instagram has watch events on 1.0% of items, so the test could never fire.
  - Before any scoring, the experimenter changed it to "≥ 50% of the platform's items carry a watch interaction", using run meta only. Under that rule Instagram has no watch data and YouTube does.
- **Does it matter? No.**
  - The amendment implements the preregistered *intent* ("watch data unknown, not false").
  - The threshold was chosen from a coverage statistic (1% vs 94%), not from labels or scores. Any threshold between about 1% and 94% gives the same split.
  - Measured effect: the preregistered rule would treat Instagram as having watch data.
