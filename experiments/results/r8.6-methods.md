## Arm definitions (as preregistered)

### Y8.6-WP · `wp-v1`

For each YouTube item: rewatches r = max(watch_count − 1, 0), g_w = min(ln(1 + r) / ln 4, 1), L = liked, Pl = in a named playlist (any collection other than "Watch later"). **m(i) = min(5, 3·g_w + 2·L + 2·Pl)** fused-rank places; Instagram items get m = 0. It is applied to the whole fused list after P's multiplier and before the refine gate: key = position − m, stable by (key, position), then the cut to 1,000. No item rises more than 5 places from its own boost or falls more than 5.

- **Index level:** 11,178 of 115,615 YouTube items (9.7%) get m > 0. The −1 on watch_count means a single watch earns nothing; that removes the advantage every `known_dev` target has by construction (each target was watched) over the 6,930 YouTube items with no watch (liked or saved only).
- **Where it fires** (label-free, per typed search, r1–r3):

| Split | Searches with ≥ 1 boosted item in the pre-boost top 20 | Boosted items in the pre-boost top 20, mean (p95) | Boosted items in the final top 10, mean |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| dev (38 typed) | 22–24 | 2.55–2.71 (10–11) | 1.42–1.71 |
| known_dev (381) | 335–337 | 3.27 (8–9) | 1.89 |
| ig_known_dev (150) | 45–49 | 0.63–0.65 (3–4) | 0.33–0.35 |

- **Leakage flag for the evaluator (fixed in the preregistration; label-free):** the counts and kinds are end-of-export. The watch that defines a `known_dev` label is itself one of the item's watches, so a re-find that was a rewatch shows as r ≥ 1 (and a like added after the search also counts). At search time in the product those later interactions would not exist yet. Any `known_dev` gain from WP therefore partly measures this. `dev` (judged relevance) and `ig_known_dev` (Instagram targets; WP never boosts Instagram items) carry no such bias; dev yt is the clean read. The architecture review (§7, X4) flags the related library-selection artefact.

### Y8.6-FL · `fl-v1`

Two BM25 indexes over YouTube items only, with the shared index's tokeniser and parameters (uni + bigrams, k1 1.2, b 0.75; own idf and mean length): **title** (100,967 docs, the Takeout title) and **channel** (100,948 docs, `owner_name`). Both are scored with the search's existing BM25 query string. YouTube items lose their shared-BM25 term (title + "by channel" + playlist names) and gain 1.0 / (60 + r_title) + 0.5 / (60 + r_channel), with tied scores sharing their mean rank. Instagram items keep their exact rank in the unchanged shared list. Playlist names leave the keyword side for YouTube; they stay in the item vectors. TI's te term is unchanged; its imputed term for non-te items uses n_O = 2.5 for YouTube (summed own-list weights), 2 for Instagram.

Label-free reads (per typed search, r1–r3):

| Split | Title list length, mean (p50) | Channel list length, mean (p50; p95) | Searches with a non-empty channel list | YouTube share of top 10: B8.5 → FL |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| dev (38 typed) | 948–956 (1,003–1,020) | 886–919 (869–911; 2,262–2,282) | 37 of 38 | 0.595 / 0.626 / 0.590 → 0.568 / 0.597 / 0.558 |
| known_dev (381) | 540–542 (407–411) | 304–310 (96–100; 1,183–1,207) | 334 of 381 | 0.939 / 0.939 / 0.939 → 0.928 / 0.926 / 0.928 |
| ig_known_dev (150) | 873–875 (1,011–1,013) | 346–356 (231–242; 1,029–1,048) | 150 of 150 | 0.238 / 0.255 / 0.243 → 0.232 / 0.225 / 0.219 |

- **The preregistered risk did not show the way I expected:** FL *lowers* the YouTube share of top 10s slightly on every split (−0.01 to −0.03), instead of raising it. A YouTube item matching both fields can get up to 1.5× its old keyword mass, but many YouTube items lose keyword credit they had from playlist names and from the shared "by …" text, and the title list's ranks are on a YouTube-only scale.
- **Channel lists are long and fire almost everywhere.** The shared tokeniser keeps short common words (two letters and up, no stop list), so nearly every search matches some channel name. A matching channel's videos all tie, and the tie rule lets a whole channel enter at its mean rank (p95 lengths over 1,000). Each such item gets at most 0.5 / (60 + mean rank), small for large channels. On `ig_known_dev` every search has a non-empty channel list.
- **FL changes far more top 10s than WP** (`known_dev` set 288 of 381). The evaluator should expect a larger |Δ| in either direction.
