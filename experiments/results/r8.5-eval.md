**Flags on Q8** (for the orchestrator):
1. **The saving is storage only.** As run, typed p50 is *worse*: 0.585–0.603 vs 0.444–0.448 s (+31–36%), and local p50 is 0.20–0.22 vs 0.07 s. Numpy upcasts int8 before multiplying, so the int8 scan is 4.6× slower than float32 (48.8 vs 10.7 ms per query vector). Adopting Q8 needs an int8 dot kernel (e.g. SQ8 / VNNI) or a dequantise-on-load fallback. The fallback keeps only the disk saving (−50% vs the float16 file), not the RAM one. The rankings do not depend on the kernel.
2. **s10_2 sits at the clause line, though the rule does not apply it there.** `known_dev` s10_2 is −0.0052 [−0.012, −0.001], 0/4; `ig_known_dev` −0.0067 [−0.018, 0.000], 0/2. Both are within noise (MDE 0.0085 / 0.014) and come from 2–6 targets leaving the top 10 over 3 repeats. Had the clause been applied to s10_2, Q8 would miss the point clause by 0.0002 and 0.0017.
3. **More-like-this reads the int8 store.** None of the 8 is identical 1,000 deep (6 on dev). All 6 dev ones score the same as B8.4.
4. **Refine re-sampling.** 106 of 227 refined searches used a new rf-v1 response. Q8's refined-subset Δ (dev −0.003, `known_dev` +0.003, `ig_known_dev` +0.022) mixes in that noise; the unrefined subsets are flat.

**Flags on QB:**
- **The dev loss comes from the candidate stage, not only from re-sampling.** Where QB's candidate stage changed a refined search's top-20 set (17 search-repeats), the Δ is −0.094. About −0.004 of the −0.015 comes from QB's q_ig shift (up to 0.12) dropping B8.4's refine on 2 search-repeats. Unrefined searches are flat (−0.0001).
- **Candidate recall.** It is 0.96 on `known_dev` (min 0.63; about 660 candidates on the one-row instant path), against ≥ 0.98 elsewhere. s10_2 drops −0.006 / −0.013 on the known sets.
- **What a retry would change.** A larger K, or a float/int8 rescoring of the union top 20 before the refine gate, might keep the scan speed. QB's storage saving is no better than Q8's (1,152 vs 1,024 B). Its value is the scan speed: Hamming top 400 takes 5.3 ms against 10.7 ms for a float32 scan.

**Flag on TS:** the ramp damps te mostly on correct YouTube reads (w = 0 below 0.35), not on the misreads it targets. The misreads keep w ≈ 0.75, so it recovers only +0.005 of the −0.035 `known_dev` loss. Meanwhile it still drops te on Instagram searches read below 0.35 (−0.144 on 6) and dampens it on reads in [0.5, 0.75). This is the same trade TG made, at about half the size. A prior read alone does not seem to separate TI's good and bad effects. Since TS's known_dev gain is +0.002 and its losses are on Instagram searches, a further q_ig-threshold arm looks low-value.
