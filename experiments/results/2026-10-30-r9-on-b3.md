## Verdicts (rule: Δ ≥ 0.03 with paired CI > 0, or Δ ≥ 0.02 with W > L and no extra cost)

- **23 · enrichment prompt te-v2b: NO.** Δ −0.025 [−0.055, +0.001], W/L/T 5/10/1, and all three repeats are negative. The loss is on typed searches (−0.034, 3/9/1), mainly name (0.541 vs 0.629), people (0.765 vs 0.887) and place (0.348 vs 0.426). R@100 rises slightly (+0.013 [+0.001, +0.026]), and MLT is +0.016 (2/1/0), so the new text pulls more relevant items into the top 100 but ranks them worse in the top 10. The single-answer group drops (nDCG 0.309 vs 0.372, MRR 0.404 vs 0.511). It also costs +$0.033 per 1k items. Caveat: provisional, because only the 5,002-item sample uses te-v2b and the rest keep te-v1c, so BM25 and dense scores mix two text styles. The sample covers B3's top 200 for every dev query, so a full-corpus pass is unlikely to reverse the sign.
- **24 · rerank with summary and kind: NO by the rule.** Δ +0.013 [+0.001, +0.026] (CI just above 0), W/L/T 6/1/9, per repeat −0.002 / +0.024 / +0.015. Δ is below 0.02, and it costs extra, so neither clause applies. Signals in its favour: few losses (1), typed +0.016 (6/1/6), the single-answer MRR is up (0.622 vs 0.511), and name and people are up. It also improves on exp 14 (+0.016 on the honest base, CI across 0), now with a CI that excludes 0. The cost is +$0.28/1k typed searches and about doubling typed-search latency (≈ 2.6 s → ≈ 5.4 s p50). For ≈ +0.013 nDCG@10, that is a poor trade for interactive search. It could suit an optional or background "precise" mode.
- **B3 on r9:** 0.570 [0.444, 0.687] (0.563–0.574), R@100 0.526; Δ vs honest base **+0.080 [+0.036, +0.133]**, 13/3/0; Δ vs B2 +0.022 [+0.008, +0.037], 10/1/5. **B3 stays the base.**
