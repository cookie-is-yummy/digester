# Round 8: the cost- and performance-optimal search architecture

*9 Nov 2026 · one founder's library (Instagram + YouTube, 121k searchable items) · loop rounds 8.1–8.8 plus one final held-out scoring. Aggregates only; no per-search detail. Sources: `experiments/BRIEF-8.md`, `results/validation-audit.md`, `docs/research/2026-11-08-architecture-review.md`, `results/r8.1-eval.md` … `r8.8-eval.md`, `r8.7-train-pool.md`, `r8-final.md`, `STATE.md`, `LOG.md`.*

- **Spend:** about $1.59 for setup, the audit and rounds 8.1–8.8 (ledger: OpenAI $2.882 → $4.368, Gemini $3.776 → $3.878). The final evaluator made no paid calls. The held-out runner's own refine and timing spend is not reported in the results files.
- **Status of every architecture change below:** Proposed (A-006). Nothing here is merged into `scrolldigest/`.

## Bottom line

1. **Recommend B8.5 ("Q8") as the round-8 search pipeline.** It is the cheapest configuration the held-out sets support:
   - **cost** $0.049 per 1,000 typed searches against $0.127 for the old base (−61%);
   - **quality** +0.110 mrr on synthetic Instagram searches (Holm 1e-4). On real YouTube searches it is level (+0.003, no loss detected);
   - **first screen** 0.58 s p50 on short YouTube searches, against 1.97 s.
2. **Don't make the gpt-5-mini refine ("FM") the default yet.** On held-out it adds +0.026 on synthetic Instagram (Holm 0.011, but below the 0.03 bar and its MDE of 0.031) and +0.008 on real YouTube (not significant). It costs +$0.043 per 1k (+86%). Decide after a real Instagram search set.
3. **YouTube quality did not move all round.** On the primary real-search set, the old base, Q8 and FM are indistinguishable (0.299 / 0.301 / 0.309 mrr). Round 8 made YouTube cheaper and faster, not better.
4. **The Instagram gain is only shown on LLM-written searches.** Those searches share wording with the te summaries that the te lists index. The bias is unresolved, not refuted. Real Instagram searches written by a person are the next thing to get.
5. **Something did go wrong in the final run, but only in its cost figures.** A timing/ledger race inflated some logged costs. Rankings are unaffected. The corrected costs are used below (section 5).

## 1. The question

BRIEF-8 asked for the **cost- and performance-optimal architecture**: the best quality per dollar and per second, run on one founder's library, judged on new, larger search sets. It also asked for two checks first: an audit of the validation system (phase A) and an architecture review (phase B).

**The sets used:**
- **`known_dev` / `known_test`:** 381 + 381 real YouTube searches (My Activity, 2019–21). Label = the video watched next.
- **`ig_known_dev` / `ig_known_test`:** 150 + 150 Instagram searches written by gemini-3.5-flash-lite from a saved post's caption, creator and te summary. Label = that post.
- **`dev`:** 44 graded searches, nDCG@10.

## 2. What the validation audit found and changed

**Found:**
- **Most past verdicts sat inside the noise.** The median per-search SD of Δ is 0.12, so the minimum detectable Δ (MDE, 80% power) is 0.12 on 10 searches, 0.075 on 21 and 0.050 on 44. None of the 30 dev passes in rounds 4.1–7 survives Holm. The only well-supported result was 3-large over 3-small (H0 over C0).
- **The AI judge is consistent with itself (κ 0.66) but only moderately close to Derrick (κ 0.39–0.43).** It is slightly stricter than he is, especially on thin items.
- **The known-item sets needed fixes.** 24% of `known_test` targets were relevant items in `known_dev` (a session leak). The scorer also counted autoplay "later" videos as hits. In a 40-pair hand check, 12.5% of labels are clear noise.
- **`ig_known` is about 3× more lexically matched than real searches,** so it favours te lists and text-reading rerankers.

**Changed (in `experiments/eval/PROTOCOL.md`):**
- Known-item metrics now use the watched target only (`mrr2`, `s10_2`).
- The primary YouTube held-out set is `known_test_clean`: the 216 session-disjoint searches.
- Every report states sd_d, MDE and Holm across the round's arms, plus grade sensitivity and the pooled share.
- Pooling covers every unjudged top-10 item.
- Held-out texts are kept out of method-side caches.

**Left Proposed, in A-005:** dev as a screen with held-out confirmation, two judges per pooled item, a Derrick calibration sheet, label repair, and an `ig_known` slice written without te text.

## 3. The architecture review's ranking (before the loop)

The review found the shape (rewrite → dense + BM25 → RRF) roughly right. Quality had come from item representation, not the query side. The LLM rewrite was about 92% of cost and 1.8 s of the 2.0 s p50.

| Rank | Option | What round 8 found |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Shape-routed planner with a no-rewrite keyword path (A1) | Built as route-r1 (R). Adopted as cheaper-equivalent; held up on held-out |
| 2 | Deterministic creator index (A2) | Determinism fixed. The creator list (H5d, CI) lost on known-item sets by displacement |
| 3 | LLM listwise refine of the top 20 (A3) | Only gated to YouTube-read rewrite searches (FY/TFY). Dev gain, not testable on held-out |
| 4 | Learned fusion from search → watch (A5) | Blocked: no disjoint training pool exists in the data |
| 5 | MCP tool surface (A4) | Not evaluable offline; not built |
| 6 | Local sparse / late interaction / quantization (A6) | int8 vectors (Q8) adopted for storage; binary + rescoring lost |

**Re-ranked after round 8:** (1) stays first and is now the base. (3) is cost-marginal. (2) is demoted. (4) waits on data. (6) is a desktop task: it needs an int8 kernel.

## 5. Final held-out result

- **Methods scored:** H0r8, Q8 and FM, once each, all together.
- **Scorings used:** `known_test` 1 of 3 and `ig_known_test` 1 of 3; 2 remain on each.
- **Metric:** mrr2. Holm runs over the 3 comparisons per set.
- **Checked:** two independent verifiers reproduced every level, Δ, CI and Holm value within 0.002.

| `ig_known_test` (150) | 0.369 / 0.479 / 0.505 | **+0.110 [+0.057, +0.162]**, Holm 1e-4 | +0.026 [+0.006, +0.049], 8/1, Holm 0.011 | +0.135 [+0.084, +0.186], Holm < 1e-4 |

**How to read it:**
- **Real YouTube (clean set):** the three methods are indistinguishable. Nothing passes Holm and no point estimate is negative.
  - On `s10_2` (co-primary), Q8 − H0r8 has CI [−0.032, +0.042], just past the −0.03 non-inferiority line. So "no loss detected" holds; non-inferiority on `s10_2` is not established.
- **Full `known_test`:** its significant results come from the 165 searches outside the clean subset, which overlap `known_dev` sessions (Q8 − H0r8 there is +0.033). Read that as leak-tilted.
  - FM − Q8 is about the same inside and outside the clean subset (+0.008 / +0.010), so the leak reading does not apply to it.
- **Synthetic Instagram:** the package's gain replicates at about 85% of the dev size (+0.110 against +0.130).
  - FM's increment replicates in direction at about 64% of the dev size (+0.026 against +0.040). Its CI contains the dev point.
  - As on dev, the increment sits on searches the prior misreads as YouTube (36 searches, +0.103).

**The cost bug (the "something wrong").**
- **What happened:** `experiments/eval/final8_heldout.py` `_timed` takes a call's cost from the *last ledger row written since the call started*. The timing passes ran at the same time as the paid refine passes, and as each other.
- **How big it is:**
  - The 3-large repeat-3 timing file has **40** entries carrying a refine call's cost, not 24 as first reported: 24 on `known_test` and 16 on `ig_known_test`.
  - The 3-small te timing files and repeat 1 carry a few more, worth under $0.002 per 1k.
  - The runner's logged `ig_known_test` means ($0.146 / $0.112 / $0.163) were overstated by $0.010–0.016.
  - H0r8's `known_test` figure was overstated by $0.003: corrected it is $0.127, not $0.128.
- **What it does not affect:** rankings and latency (each call's own seconds).
- **Fix for future runners:** take the cost from the ledger row with the call's own purpose, or never run timing alongside paid calls.

## 6. Recommended architecture (B8.5 = Q8)

One index for both platforms, with a code planner in front and an optional refine behind:

1. **Planner (route-r1, no LLM).**
   - Short searches with no description, question or time/behaviour words take the **instant path**: the raw query → 3-large dense + BM25 → RRF. That is 86% of real YouTube searches.
   - Searches naming a library creator, or repeating the user's own item text, also take the instant path.
   - The rest take the **rewrite path**: the H0 recipe, with the gpt-5-nano qr-v1 rewrite, HyDE, PRF, BM25 and RRF.
2. **Platform prior (prior-p1).** A soft per-platform weight built from the router's text guess, the platform share of the search's own top 20, the user's platform split and matched creators. It is never a filter.
3. **te lists for Instagram items only (TI)**, BM25 + 3-small dense over the te-v1c text, with coverage-fair fusion. YouTube titles get no te.
4. **Refine:** one gpt-5-nano listwise call over the top 20, only on rewrite-routed searches the prior reads as YouTube (about 12% of held-out typed searches). It reorders; it never adds items.
5. **Item vectors:** 3-large 1,024 d, stored as per-dimension int8.

**Figures:**

| | Q8 |
| --- | --- |
| Cost per 1k typed searches | **$0.049** on the held-out mix. Range $0.029 (keyword-heavy YouTube) to $0.102 (descriptive Instagram); dev mix $0.055 |
| One-time cost per 1k items | 3-large embedding about $0.004; te pass $0.18 per 1k Instagram items (Batch, A-003) |
| Latency, first screen | p50 0.58 s on keyword searches; 2.05 s on rewrite-heavy Instagram searches (H0r8: 1.97 s) |
| Latency, refined screen | p50 3.50 s, on refined searches only; total p95 is +1.3 s over H0r8 |
| Item-vector storage | **1,024 B per item** (int8 + scales): −75% against float32 in RAM (4,096 B), −50% against today's float16 file. 124 MB for 121k items |
| Other per-item storage | te text (about 1.1 KB per Instagram item, A-003) plus its 3-small vector and the BM25 index. Not re-measured in round 8 |

**Local / desktop fit:**
- Search itself is local (0.1–0.3 s).
- Every search still makes one 3-large query-embedding call, plus a 3-small call on the te path. Rewrite-path searches add a cloud LLM call.
- int8 is **slower** as run (numpy upcasts: 48.8 ms against 10.7 ms per scan, about +0.13 s local p50). The desktop needs an int8 dot kernel (e.g. VNNI). Without one, only the disk saving holds, not the RAM saving.
- A fully offline desktop also needs a local query embedder. None has been tested against 3-large.

**Privacy fit:**
- **Better than H0r8:** the instant path sends no LLM call. 86% of real YouTube searches never reach a model provider (beyond the embedding API).
- **Vault data** (the platform split, matched creators) is used at query time only.
- **The refine** sends the query plus 20 public candidate texts to the provider, the same exposure class as the rewrite. Friends' slots are fixed and never sent.
- **The te pass** is A-003's: collection names must stay out of the shared store.
- **Open gap:** `05-processor.md` still has no written privacy section (shared store vs vault, key revocation). A-006 item 6 asks for one.

**What held-out can't vouch for inside Q8:**
- **The nano refine** (+$0.012 per 1k, +1.2–1.3 s p95) rests on dev nDCG alone (TFY +0.038, Holm 0.021, below its MDE). On held-out refined YouTube searches, Q8 − H0r8 is −0.038 (23, clean) and −0.018 (43, full), both within noise. Keeping it is a founder call (section 8).
- **The pieces were not scored apart on held-out.** The +0.110 Instagram gain belongs to the package (prior + te + routing + refine), not to any one part.

## 8. What the founders must decide or provide

1. **A-006 (both founders).** The round-8 evidence is now recorded per item in `docs/amendments.md`.
   - Accept the planner, the platform prior and Instagram-only te lists in direction.
   - Defer the creator index.
   - Treat the refine as optional.
   - Decide whether to keep the nano refine in the default (dev evidence only, +1.3 s p95) and whether to offer FM (+$0.043 per 1k).
2. **A-005 (Derrick).** The held-out run supports round 8's "adopt on one set, non-inferior on the rest" practice in direction: every `ig_known_dev` gain replicated, at 64–85% of its dev size. But every one was confirmed only on a set with the same bias. Accept or amend the adoption rule before any round 9.
3. **Real Instagram known-item searches.** About 30–40, written by Derrick without seeing te summaries. This is the only way to settle the te bias and FM.
4. **A Derrick calibration sheet** of 60–80 blind items (A-005 item 3). The AI judge sits at κ 0.39–0.43 against him, and no held-out graded set exists for the dev nDCG claims. A fresh graded set of 40–45 searches would test them.
5. **The raw My Activity search history** (`MyActivity.html` or `searches.jsonl`), or product click logs, to unblock learned fusion. The 762 known searches use every pair the current data can produce.
6. **An int8 dot kernel** (or a dequantise-on-load fallback) before the desktop build, and a local query-embedder trial if the desktop must run offline.
7. **Housekeeping:** fix `_timed` before the next held-out run. Add a LOG row for this final scoring; none exists yet.
